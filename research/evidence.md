# Evidence record for Draft 1

Prepared 24 September 2026. The citation keys below match `references.bib`, which contains the full titles, dates, and source URLs. This is a research companion, not part of the 
submitted essay's page count.

Updated 1 October 2026 for Section 0, based on repository head `8fbd73ad2cf8fecc583f8d1934ad60458bbf3309`.

## What the evidence establishes

1. FY2026 enactments were much higher than the president requested for NSF and NASA Science. This supports the claim that congressional intervention preserved funding.
2. NSF's appropriation remained well below its CHIPS and Science authorization. Preventing a proposed reduction and fulfilling a previously authorized growth path are different 
achievements.
3. The FY2027 NASA request disproportionately reduced Science while increasing Exploration. The agency-wide reduction conceals those priorities.
4. The House committee's May FY2027 proposal also reduced NSF and NASA Science. Congress cannot be treated as a single consistently pro-research actor.
5. Administrative actions can interrupt projects after Congress appropriates money. The documented NSF episode and the NIH impoundment decision establish different aspects of this 
problem; they are not interchangeable legal findings.
6. Tham (2023) supports operational disruption but does not establish a statistically significant publication loss. Tham et al. (2026) separately estimates employment effects. These outcomes and studies must not be conflated; neither supplies a conversion from NSF cuts to lost discoveries.
7. Section 0 defines the crisis in terms of continuity and threatened research capacity. Historical cancellation counts establish interruptions, while later reinstatements prevent treating them as current net losses. This definition is the essay's analytical choice, not a standardized statistical threshold.

## Source-by-source findings and limits

### `mazuzan` -- NSF's institutional history

Read the official historical narrative, especially Chapters I–III. It describes the Bush–Kilgore disagreement, Truman's 1947 veto, NSF's creation in 1950, and subsequent Cold War 
growth. Supports the historical claim that scientific autonomy and political accountability were negotiated together. This agency history is useful background; it does not measure 
the effects of current funding policy.

### `constitution` -- Appropriations Clause

Article I, section 9, clause 7, in the National Archives transcript, conditions Treasury withdrawals on appropriations made by law. Supports the constitutional foundation of 
congressional spending control. The provision alone does not answer every dispute over an agency's discretion to administer grants.

### `chips2022` -- Public Law 117-167

Division B, section 10303, authorizes NSF funding for FY2023–FY2027. The exact FY2026 amount is $17,832,420,000. The chart follows the CRS table's rounded $17,832 million. These 
are authorizations, not appropriated funds or outlays. The authorization gap is therefore an unfulfilled policy benchmark, not a withdrawal of cash already available to NSF.

### `harris2026` -- CRS R48783, 6 January 2026

Tables 1, 6, and A-1 supply NSF budget comparisons and the authorized path. Table 1 and its discussion distinguish FY2025 enacted $9.060 billion from $8.826 billion after 
excluding $234 million in emergency construction funding that was not made available. The FY2026 request is $3,903.2 million. The report predates the final FY2026 appropriation; 
that final amount comes from Public Law 119-74.

Table A-1 and its notes report FY2023 total funding including supplements as $9.877 billion. The CSV uses that reported total rather than reconstructing it from separately rounded 
components. The FY2024 request uses the table's discretionary amount, $11.315 billion; it is not combined with an additional mandatory proposal. Sources and accounting choices are 
retained in the CSV.

### `congress2026` -- Public Law 119-74, 23 January 2026

Division A, Title III, supplies NASA Science's $7.250 billion and NSF's six accounts. NSF totals $8.750 billion: research $7,176.50 million; major facilities $251 million; STEM 
education $938.25 million; operations $355 million; National Science Board $5.09 million; inspector general $24.16 million. Sections 505 and 528 address reprogramming and spending 
plans. Enactment establishes budget authority, not that all funds have been obligated or paid out.

### `nasa2025` -- FY2026 NASA Budget Estimates

Printed page BUD-1, PDF page 2, provides the FY2025 enacted and FY2026 requested baselines. NASA Science: $7,334.2 million enacted versus $3,907.6 million requested. NASA total: 
$24,838.3 million versus $18,809.1 million. FY2025 science-division cells are blank in this table; the dataset leaves those cells blank rather than substituting FY2024 figures. 
Printed FY2026 division requests sum to $0.1 million more than the printed Science total because of rounding; the chart uses the reported total.

### `nasa2026` -- FY2027 NASA Budget Estimates

Printed BUD-1, PDF page 2, supplies FY2026 enacted and FY2027 requested accounts. NASA total: $24,438.3 million versus $18,829.1 million. Science: $7,250 million versus $3,893.9 
million. Exploration: $7,783 million versus $8,513.9 million. FY2027 science divisions also come from this table. BUD-6 reports separate multiyear mandatory funding; it is 
excluded from the regular budget comparisons. Consequently, the figure is not a complete measure of all human-spaceflight resources.

### `appropriators2026` -- FY2026 joint explanatory statement

Printed pages 52–53 allocate Science's $7,250 million among Earth Science ($2,153 million), Planetary Science ($2,541.2 million), Astrophysics ($1,595 million), Heliophysics 
($874.8 million), and Biological and Physical Sciences ($86 million). These values supply Figure 2's FY2026 division baselines, which the FY2027 agency summary does not fill in. 
Page 53 specifies Roman at $300 million and LISA at $80.5 million.

Pages 1–3 discuss spending plans, reprogramming, and workforce notifications. These are congressional directions and allocations, not a dataset of agency expenditures. A joint 
explanatory statement should not be described as identical to statutory text in every legal respect. Mission allocations also require life-cycle interpretation: completing 
construction can reduce a line without reducing its scientific ambition.

### `omb2026` -- FY2027 President's Budget

The NASA chapter, printed pages 67–69, argues for spending restraint, commercial approaches, and selected mission priorities, and criticizes Mars Sample Return's cost trajectory. 
Used to represent the administration's stated rationale fairly. Its descriptions of waste and low priority are the administration's judgments, not independently established 
efficiency findings.

### `senate2026` -- Senate Appropriations majority release

The 15 January release reports an 82–15 Senate vote and a 397–28 House vote and presents Collins's and Moran's arguments for the package. Supports claims about bipartisan passage 
and these officials' expressed priorities. A press release is evidence of a public position, not a causal account of every legislator's motives.

### `murray2026` -- Murray's Senate office release

The 15 January release frames the settlement as rejecting major cuts and reasserting congressional direction. Supports the paper's comparison of political arguments. It is a 
partisan primary source, so the paper attributes the position rather than adopting every evaluative claim as fact.

### `house2026` -- House FY2027 committee draft

The report prepared for the 13 May markup recommends $6 billion for NASA Science (printed p. 91) and $7 billion for NSF (p. 107). Relative to FY2026, these are cuts of 17.2% and 
20.0%. This is a dated committee proposal, not a final FY2027 law or a claim about the most recent negotiating position. It is included as counterevidence to portraying Congress 
as uniformly protective of science.

### `aas2026` -- American Astronomical Society advocacy

The January article celebrates congressional funding and asks members to thank legislators. Supports the characterization of scientific societies as participating interest groups. 
It cannot establish how much advocacy changed appropriations or votes. Budget figures in the essay are taken from government tables and legislation, not the society's summaries.

### `nsfpriorities2025` -- NSF priorities and FAQs

The 18 April statement changes the agency's treatment of broader impacts. Subsequent FAQs describe 114 awards to 45 institutions reinstated on 30 June following a preliminary 
injunction. Supports the existence of interruptions and judicial constraints in this specific episode. These are not counts of all terminations, a current net tally, or evidence 
that every affected award was in physics. A preliminary injunction is not a final merits judgment.

### `gao2025` -- GAO decision B-337203

The 5 August decision concludes that NIH's withholding of grant funds violated the Impoundment Control Act and discusses legislative rescission as the appropriate route for 
proposed cancellation. Supports the institutional argument about executing appropriations. It is an official GAO decision about NIH, not a court ruling about NSF and not a finding 
on every individual grant termination.

### `tham2023` -- Peer-reviewed study of grant interruptions

Read the article's methods, spending results, and research-output discussion. A difference-in-differences design compares interrupted and uninterrupted NIH renewals, with separate 
analyses for laboratories with one R01 and multiple grants. It finds operational spending disruption and buffering from additional grants. For publication estimates, the article 
states: “None of the estimates are statistically different from zero.” This null result is retained in the essay. The study does not estimate the effect of NSF cuts on 
cosmological discoveries; that connection is explicitly an inference.

### `azoulay2019` -- Peer-reviewed public/private research study

The published abstract describes variation from NIH funding rules and reports increased private-sector patenting following additional public funding. Supports complementarity 
between public and private research. The published estimate is 2.7 additional patents per $10 million, whereas an earlier working-paper version reported a different estimate. The 
essay avoids the numerical estimate and makes no return-on-investment projection for astrophysics. This is biomedical patent evidence, not a measure of all scientific value.

### `balzer2026` -- NASA description of Roman's planned survey

The 10 February article describes using weak lensing and galaxy clustering to investigate dark matter and dark energy. Supports the concrete cosmology example. These are planned 
scientific investigations, not observations already completed. The essay does not depend on a forecast launch date.

### `nsb2025` -- Science and Engineering Indicators

The Academic R&D section and Figure DISC-11 report approximately $38.1 billion in federal funding out of $68.5 billion of academic basic research in 2023. The calculated share is 
about 56%. The denominator is academic basic research, not all U.S. R&D, all university spending, or all federal science funding.

## Calculations and boundaries

### Sources added for Section 0 (checked 1 October 2026)

**`gaonsf2025` — direct government record.** Read the full report's online Appendix IV, discussion after table 31: https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107576/index.html. GAO reports NSF's publicly listed terminations across directorates, rather than only awards at minority-serving institutions. The count is a June 2025 snapshot, not a current portfolio count. GAO attributes the terminations to agency priorities; it does not establish that each project failed scientific review or measure astrophysics losses.

**`oliveira2026` — peer-reviewed descriptive study.** Read the complete article through Europe PMC's full-text XML: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC13037894/fullTextXML. Methods include 2,291 of 2,295 listed grants with award-size information. Distinguish remaining funding from original award value. The discussion explicitly limits downstream causal claims. Do not import its multiplier-based economic projections as observed losses, or infer group-specific termination probabilities without the complete at-risk portfolio.

**`tham2026` — peer-reviewed employment study.** Publication date, authors, DOI and published abstract verified against publisher-deposited Crossref metadata: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1162/rest.a.1865. Census also lists the article as accepted/forthcoming: https://www.census.gov/topics/research/research-transparency-public-access/open-science/articles/forthcoming.html. The publisher's full text returned HTTP 403; the essay uses only the published abstract's design and single-R01 nonemployment result. Three percentage points is an absolute change (the abstract reports 40% relatively). It concerns earlier NIH renewals, not 2025 cancellations or astrophysics. No new analysis of the underlying restricted personnel records was performed.

### Existing budget calculations

All CSV monetary amounts are nominal millions of U.S. dollars of budget authority or congressional allocations. `analysis/make_figures.py` generates the two PDF/PNG figures and 
writes the precise calculations to `analysis/derived_metrics.json` and `analysis/nasa_changes.csv`.

- Percentage change: `100 * (new / baseline - 1)`.
- Authorized funding share: `100 * appropriation / authorization`.
- Proposed reduction offset: `100 * (enacted - requested) / (prior_enacted - requested)`.
- NSF adjusted FY2025 baseline: `9060 - 234 = 8826` million.
- NASA hierarchy: divisions are part of Science; Science and Exploration are part of NASA. Never sum the displayed rows.
- Figures exclude inflation adjustment, outlays, award-level disbursements, and NASA's separate mandatory funding.
- No regression, significance test, or confidence interval is fitted to these exact administrative comparisons. The data do not identify the causal effect of funding on 
discoveries or measure scientific productivity.

Eight government PDFs were downloaded and read. `source_manifest.csv` records their URLs and SHA-256 hashes. Four selected table-page images are retained in `source_tables/` to 
make key transcriptions reviewable. Full online sources remain linked in the bibliography; the project does not bundle copyrighted research articles.

## Conclusions versus recommendations

Congress's FY2026 preservation of funds and the remaining authorization gap are direct descriptive findings. The claim that interruptions threaten a working research system draws 
on the documented administrative episodes and the NIH study, with explicit limits. Bridge funding, transparent implementation reporting, and independent scientific review are the 
author's recommendations; the sources do not experimentally establish that this exact combined policy would succeed.
