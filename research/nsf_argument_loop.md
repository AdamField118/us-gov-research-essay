## Argument decisions

- Principal case: NSF's FY2017–FY2026 funding record, with FY2026 as the budget confrontation.
- Claim: preventing most proposed cuts is a significant congressional achievement, yet historical purchasing power and Congress's authorized expansion goals reveal the limits of that defense.
- Three comparisons have different jobs: request versus enactment measures the budget settlement; inflation adjustment measures broad purchasing power; authorization versus appropriation measures distance from a legislative objective.
- Counterargument: legislatures can change priorities and need not preserve historical budgets or fully appropriate authorizations. The response defends explicit justification and program-level accountability rather than an automatic entitlement to past purchasing power or CHIPS targets.
- NASA provides a limited comparison showing why agency totals require a statement of the activities protected. It is not a second thesis about the optimal space program.
- NSF terminations and NIH evidence connect appropriation to implementation. They do not quantify NSF employment losses.
- Removed the separate histories of science administration, NIH patenting, Roman mission case, and broad menu of bridge funding/multiyear policies. Retained a focused proposal to connect comparative budget explanations with spending plans and award reporting.
